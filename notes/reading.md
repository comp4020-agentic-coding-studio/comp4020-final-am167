# Reading: what "good" means at small scale

The final project brief asks for writing on "the small web, games made for a
handful of friends, tools built for one workshop": "Read some of it, cite it
in your README, and discuss your position in `PROCESS.md`." These are the
agent's summaries, fetched 2026-10-04 to help me choose; skim the originals
before citing. Ranked by how well they fit Kessler.

## 1. Clay Shirky, "Situated Software" (2004): best fit

<https://gwern.net/doc/technology/2004-03-30-shirky-situatedsoftware.html>
(original: <http://shirky.com/essays/situated-software/>, didn't load)

- Software "designed for use by a specific social group, rather than for a
  generic set of 'users'", with "a refusal to embrace scale, generality or
  completeness as unqualified virtues".
- The group does work the code doesn't: "the designer can simply assume the
  group has a certain capability, without needing to recapitulate it in
  code."
- Example: a student money app with no escrow; it just made non-payers
  visible ("Deadbeat alert. Deal with it.") and let the community embarrass
  them.

**For Kessler:** close to its argument. No moderation or voting; make visible
who crowded the low band and whose launches made the debris (C10 lineage),
and let the shared view govern. Pairs with Ostrom (commons survive when users
can see what each takes). → README, "What good means here".

## 2. Darius Kazemi, *Run Your Own Social* (2019, updated 2024)

<https://runyourown.social/>

- Small communities work because of limits: "no more than 50 to 100 active
  users" (his experience, not research).
- "software does not have to scale"; rules should be specific enough to be
  "actively repulsive to some people".

**For Kessler:** the case for the 200 cap and one satellite each. Tension for
`PROCESS.md`: his communities are screened friends, Kessler's sky is
strangers, so the rules (not the people) are the boundary. → README (limits)
and `PROCESS.md`.

## 3. Robin Sloan, "An app can be a home-cooked meal" (2020)

<https://www.robinsloan.com/notes/home-cooked-app/>

- An app for his family of four: "It is ruthlessly simple; we love it; no one
  else will ever use it." No logins, because it knows its users.
- "There will be no sudden redesign, no flood of ads, no pivot".

**For Kessler:** looser. Backs no accounts and "what you do stays", but
Kessler isn't for people you know, so it's more contrast than support. →
`PROCESS.md`.

## 4. Maggie Appleton, "Home-Cooked Software and Barefoot Developers" (2024)

<https://maggieappleton.com/home-cooked-software>

- Local software for "local problems for local people", increasingly built
  by non-professionals with language models; builds on Shirky and Sloan.

**For Kessler:** weak for the README (it's about who builds, not what's
good); useful in `PROCESS.md` given the course builds with agents.
