# Kessler

One sky, shared by everyone who visits. You launch a satellite carrying a
message of up to 140 characters, its beacon, and whoever has the sky open hears
it as it passes over one of three ground stations. Crowding the low band to be
heard more causes collisions, which turn both messages into static that takes
airtime from everyone. Nothing resets, and the record keeps who caused what. So what's
worth saying, if saying it costs everyone a little?

Crit 9 version.

## What good means here

Low orbit is a commons. Kessler and Cour-Palais (1978) showed debris can breed
faster than drag clears it; Hardin (1968) argues such a commons is doomed;
Ostrom (1990) found commons survive when users can see the resource and what
each takes. Shirky (2004) says the same of small software: make behaviour
visible and let the group do the rest. Kessler does: no moderation, just a
record of what each launch cost. Kazemi (2019) says small communities hold
through limits; for strangers, the sky's rules are the limit.

1. **Being heard needs other people.** A beacon is heard only by others with
   the sky open as it passes a station.
2. **Being heard more has a cost.** Low orbits pass a station every 3 minutes,
   high every 24, so being heard more means flying where it's crowded.
3. **The sky remembers.** Any object's card shows who launched it and what its
   debris destroyed.
4. **Nothing resets.** The sky heals only by decay: hours low, days high.

## What is enforced and what is judged


- a launch reaches other sessions within a second, even without JavaScript
- a minute between launches; at most 200 satellites up
- "heard by" counts people, not owners or tabs
- fragments carry every word of both messages, and trace back to operators
- a restarted server catches up to the same sky

Judged, not tested:

- **Whether it makes anyone think.** I judge by playing; crits tell me more.
- **Whether people crowd low.** Launch counts per band will show it.
- **Whether collisions feel earned.** Only watching tells me; most are still
  staged.

## What I chose not to build

- **Required accounts.** Launching takes seconds; a handle is optional. The
  cost: two browsers are two people.
- **One satellite each.** The commons is tested by how much each person takes,
  so one person can crowd everyone.
- **Staged collisions between people.** Staging uses derelicts, so the server
  never destroys someone's satellite on purpose.
