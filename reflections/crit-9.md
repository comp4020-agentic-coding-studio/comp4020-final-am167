# Crit 9 reflection

## What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?

Realising the app had no reason for a visitor to care. Partway through, everything
worked: collisions, debris, a record of who did what. But playing it, I was
just watching a simulation. The agent pointed out that I'd explained why the
project exists, but never why someone should launch. That changed what I
built next. Messages are now heard by real people, and when two satellites
collide, both owners' words are lost to static. Putting something of yours at
risk is what made a collision matter.

## What did this work change about who I want to be as a software developer?

I stopped treating a passing test suite as proof the work was done. Several
times this crit everything was green and the agent's numbers looked fine, yet
the sky felt off when I actually sat and watched it, and a panel I'd shipped
was confusing to read. Nothing automated flagged those problems; I had to.
I want to keep using what I build before calling it finished, since judging
whether something feels right to a person is my job, not the agent's.
